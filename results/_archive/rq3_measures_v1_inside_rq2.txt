==============================================================================
VERDICT — RUN 2
==============================================================================

  1. The RF score and uninsured_share correlate at Pearson +0.16540990
     across the 275 banks. The RF and OLS scores agree at Spearman +0.68145928.

  2. In the plain H3 link (event FE, bank-clustered), the three measures
     do NOT behave alike:

       RF score (leakage-free)    delta1 = +0.06453567   p = 0.296506  (n.s.)
       OLS score (same split)     delta1 = +0.01576136   p = 0.723509  (n.s.)
       uninsured_share alone      delta1 = -0.01813953   p = 0.000463  ***

     uninsured_share is significant here (p = 0.000463) with the H3-predicted
     NEGATIVE sign, while the RF score is not (p = 0.296506). The published RQ3
     null is stated for the ML score; it does not extend to uninsured_share,
     whose level effect on CAR is real and was already visible in the
     earlier interaction report.

  3. In the two-way FE interaction the RF score gives b = -0.38507629
     (p 0.022081), the OLS score b = -0.26387427 (p 0.013707),
     and uninsured_share b = -0.00168463 (p 0.939323).

  4. Entered together, S x RF score keeps p = 0.018414 and S x uninsured_share
     p = 0.794241.

  READ: in the INTERACTION, the fitted score carries something that
  uninsured_share alone does not. It survives with uninsured_share in the
  same model, while S x uninsured_share stays at p = 0.7942.

  But this is NOT a win for machine learning specifically. The OLS score,
  built from the same features and the same split, gives b = -0.26387427
  (p 0.013707) — the same result. The two scores agree at
  Spearman +0.6815. What matters is using a FITTED composite of the
  balance sheet rather than one ratio; whether that composite comes from a
  random forest or from OLS makes little difference here.

  Two further cautions, both mandatory when quoting this:
    * the sign is NEGATIVE, i.e. the OPPOSITE of what H2 predicts;
    * RUN 1's diagnostics apply in full — the calendar placebo cannot
      separate S from time, and dropping the 9 custodian banks cuts the
      RF coefficient by roughly two thirds and takes it below 5%.

  Caveat that applies to every line above: the ML score's own transfer fit
  to the listed banks is R2 = 0.0302 with SD 0.014 (scores compressed ~3.7x
  against the in-population CV version). A measure that weak cannot be
  expected to dominate a raw balance-sheet ratio, and does not.
